# CHANGELOG

All notable changes to `reservoarr.py`. Each version's invariants are earned by a real production failure — read this before changing the script.

## [6.6.0] — 2026-10-29

The release is paced on the content time it carries instead of on a 90-second average byte rate ([@sm0keb0x](https://github.com/sm0keb0x), [discussion #46](https://github.com/brko7/reservoarr/discussions/46)). **This changes pacing for every install**, on every channel whose bitrate varies, which is most of them. No new tunables; the ffmpeg command and the telemetry line format are unchanged.

- **A channel whose bitrate varies is no longer fed below realtime when it gets denser.** `Pacer.pace()` released bytes at `crate`, the content rate averaged over the last ~90s of content, so how much content per second reached the player depended on how the current stretch compared with that average. Lighter content went out faster than realtime, and the reservoir emptied into the player; denser content went out slower, and the player's buffer emptied into the reservoir. On TiviMate and iMPlayer that showed as micro-freezes after 6.5.0 had removed the 0.97 floor, with a log that looked healthy because `out` matched `crate`. The capture that showed it: the channel's bitrate fell from ~8.5 to ~3 Mbps (`crate` 8.55, 7.63, 6.72, 5.78, 4.57, 3.73, 3.17 over 90s, `vholes=0%`, the feed still arriving in realtime), the cushion went to 0, and on the last line 2.0MB covering about 1s sat in the reservoir while `out` was 3.33 Mbps: the bitrate had come back, and the player was getting ~40% of realtime. On a bench edge that sends the same drop (8.7 → 3.1 → 8.7 Mbps) in realtime, `crate` fell 7.97, 7.03, 6.09, 5.16, 4.21, 3.27, nearly the same, and when the bitrate came back the player fell 29s behind over the next 90s while the log showed the cushion climbing from 0 to 29s. Ordinary swings do the same on a smaller scale: with 15s stretches alternating at 11 and 6 Mbps, the player lost up to 3.5s of its buffer in each dense stretch. Paced on content time, the player's lead stayed within 0.25s on the drop and 0.18s on the swings (bench, macOS).

  Each slice is still charged its byte-rate time when it goes out, so the release stays smooth between PCRs. When the parser's PCR clock moves past it, the charge is trued up to the content time between the two marks, bounded to ¼–4× the byte-rate time of the bytes in between and to 0.25s over it: a garbage PCR that gets through the parser's plausibility window costs at most a quarter of a second, never a sleep on the timestamp (invariant #1, reworded). Without the 0.25s cap, one accepted garbage sample at the end of a run of rejected PCRs could cost ~3s. A clock that does not move for 1s of bytes (a run of rejected PCRs, a lost PCR PID) is not trued up: those bytes go out at the byte rate. Before the PCR locks, pacing is on bytes alone, as before. A flush is not trued up: the content it skipped never went out. The released content position is taken from the same snapshot as the cushion. `release_factor()`, the floor, the headstart and the 1.15× ceiling are unchanged; the factor now applies to content time. `crate` keeps its meaning in the log, but `out` now follows the content, so on a channel with a varying bitrate it sits over `crate` while the bitrate is above the average and under it while below; only over several minutes does it match.

  The parser's PCR marks are now keyed on the end of the packet that carries the PCR, not on the end of the 94KB chunk it arrived in. Every PCR of a chunk used to share one byte offset, which only coarsened the cushion reading; paced on those marks, the release position moved in chunk-sized jumps, 0.75s of content at 1 Mbps, past what a true-up may add. `cushion=` and `crate` read a little more exactly as a side effect.

- `tools/cdn_sim.py --pcr` advances the edge at the capture's content time instead of a fixed byte rate, which a VBR capture needs. New fixture `fixtures/vbr.ts` (`tools/make_vbr_ts.sh`, built by `just fixture`: 3 Mbps for 45s, 1 Mbps for 40s, 3 Mbps for 60s). The e2e harness now reads reservoarr's output through a pipe, as Dispatcharr does, and keeps when each byte arrived; `player_lead()` turns that into the player's lead over realtime at every output PCR. New e2e test `tests/e2e/test_vbr_pacing.py`: the lead may swing by less than 1.5s across the drop; on macOS it swung 13.9s on 6.5.0 and swings 0.3s now. The harness fails a run whose output pipe stays open after reservoarr exits instead of reading a cut-short output. Unit tests 254 → 266: the release takes as long as the content at 0.5–2× the average density, the ¼–4× bound, a 9s garbage PCR jump costing under 0.2s, the same jump after 0.95s of rejects costing under 0.3s, a flush not trued up, PCR marks at their packet's offset across chunk splits, a stuck clock falling back to the byte rate without a burst after it, and the release factor applied to content time. `docs/INVARIANTS.md` #1, `docs/TELEMETRY.md` (`crate`, "What healthy looks like"), `docs/TUNABLES.md` and the README (healthy values, "Micro-stutters every so often", "How it works") updated.

## [6.5.0] — 2026-10-25

The release floor becomes a tunable and its default goes from 0.97 to 1.0, and the pacer stops losing the time each sleep overruns ([@fadetoblah](https://github.com/fadetoblah), [@sm0keb0x](https://github.com/sm0keb0x), [discussion #46](https://github.com/brko7/reservoarr/discussions/46)). **This changes pacing for every install:** while the cushion is under `RESV_TARGET_S`, reservoarr now releases at the content rate instead of 3–5% below it. The ffmpeg command and the telemetry line format are unchanged.

- **New `RESV_FLOOR` tunable, default `1.0`: the player is never fed below realtime.** Until now `release_factor()` held the release at no less than 0.97 × the content rate once `RESV_GRACE_S` (45s) was over, and the controller asks for less than that whenever the cushion is more than 10% under the target, so on any tune whose front-load fell short of the target the player got 0.97s of content per second until the cushion caught up. Every second banked that way comes out of the player's own buffer. Two users on clients with small live buffers (TiviMate, Emby, iMPlayer and FredTV; Channels on Apple TV) saw micro-freezes from a few minutes into each tune, with the player's cache near empty and Channels showing the stream at ~0.95–0.97×, while `delaybuf.log` looked healthy: in a 1-second capture during the freezes the cushion held 11–15s against a 40s target, every error counter stayed at zero, and `out` was 0.956–0.967 × `crate` on all sixteen lines. With `RESV_HEADSTART_S=3` the headstart runs out after about 45 + 3 / 0.03 ≈ 2.5 min, the "after 2–3 min" reported. From a 10s cushion to a 30s target the player loses ~20s that way over about ten minutes, and again after every gap that drains the cushion. Lowering `RESV_TARGET_S` to 5 stopped it for one of them; on earlier releases `RESV_GRACE_S=inf` keeps the floor at 1.0 for the whole stream and does the same without bleeding the cushion down to 5s.

  The floor pays off only behind a tuner that dies on input silence with a client that buffers deeply enough to absorb the deficit (Plex Live TV), on a CDN that does not rebuild the cushion by itself. On the production Plex install it barely did anything: across its telemetry from June to September 2026 (4,246 stats lines, about 17.7h of streaming over 17 days) the cushion read 27s or more in 86% of the lines and the floor banked ~177s in all. In the eleven mid-stream recoveries from under 15s to 25s or more, each within 1–3.5 min, it contributed 0–5.5s; the rest came from the CDN's catch-up bursts and reconnect front-loads, which bank at 1.0 as well. `RESV_FLOOR=0.97` restores the old behaviour. A decimal above 0 and at most 1 is accepted; anything else (`0`, `1.5`, `0,97`, `inf`) is logged once at startup and read as `1.0`. `RESV_GRACE_S` now matters only with a floor under 1.0.

- **The pacer pays back oversleep: it released under the rate it was asked for, since v5.** `Pacer.pace()` slept off its debt and then set the debt to zero, so whatever `time.sleep()` overran was never charged, and the release ran under its target by the overrun over the slice period. `time.sleep()` runs 0.23ms long (median of 12ms sleeps) on the production Linux host and 3.6ms (40ms sleeps) on macOS; a 12 KB slice is 12ms of an 8 Mbps channel, so about 2% under there. Above the target the controller made up for it by raising the factor; under the target, at the floor, nothing did, so it added to the floor: `out` read 0.956–0.967 × `crate` in the capture from #46 against a 0.97 floor. On the bench (macOS, 2.4 Mbps, a 10s front-load against the 30s target) `out` was 0.92 × `crate` with the floor at 1.0 and 0.90 at 0.97. The debt now stays after a sleep and the next call takes the whole sleep off it: on the same bench `out` is 1.000 × `crate` at the default and 0.967–0.971 × at `RESV_FLOOR=0.97` after the grace, and the cushion holds the 5s the headstart leaves instead of creeping from 6s to 13s within 90s on lost time. A sleep that overruns by a lot (the process descheduled) is caught up by releasing the next slices without a sleep, within the 2s idle bound as before.

- `docs/TUNABLES.md` (`RESV_FLOOR`, `RESV_GRACE_S`, "When to bump what"), `docs/TELEMETRY.md` and the README updated: `out` within a percent or two of `crate` at or under the target is healthy, `out` held a few percent under it is the player fed below realtime; a new troubleshooting entry, "Micro-stutters every so often, the log looks healthy", and a third cause under "Cushion never reaches the target". `tests/unit/test_pacing.py` goes from 7 unit tests to 34 (the default never under 1.0 at any cushion, `RESV_FLOOR=0.97` after and during the grace and at its edge, a floor under the controller's 0.7, accepted and rejected values, the startup line logged once and only for an invalid value, and the pacer on a clock whose sleeps overrun by 0, 0.25 and 3.6ms: the last two fail on 6.4.0). New e2e test `tests/e2e/test_release_rate.py`: `out` against `crate` after the grace on the real pipeline, at either floor. `test_baseline_builds_cushion` now expects the front-load minus the headstart (≥18s) instead of ≥20s: with nothing banked beyond the front-load it reads ~19.9s, and the old threshold held only because the floor and the lost oversleep added a few seconds.

## [6.4.0] — 2026-10-14

Five fixes from a code review, and the script's shared state moved into classes. **No new tunables, the ffmpeg command and the telemetry line format are unchanged.** What changes at runtime: the stall watchdog cuts a silent connection when it trips, the reservoir stays on the 188-byte packet grid across connection seams, the stats line keeps coming while the reservoir is empty, a dead ffmpeg ends the stream, and the logged edge never carries credentials.

- **The stall watchdog (#4) now cuts a silent connection; before, it waited for the 30s socket timeout like everything else.** `RESV_STALL_S=25` was documented as tripping "sooner than urlopen's 30s timeout", but the forced reconnect called `response.close()` from the watchdog thread, and Python's buffered reader holds its lock for the whole blocked `read()`: `close()` waited for that lock, i.e. for the socket timeout. The `upstream stalled` line appeared at 25s, the reconnect happened at ~30s after the last byte, as without the watchdog. Shown on a local edge that sends 10 packets and goes silent: `close()` from a second thread left the read blocked until the 8s test timeout; `shutdown(SHUT_RDWR)` on the socket ended it in under 0.1s, over HTTP and HTTPS. The forced reconnect now shuts the socket down (called through `socket.socket`, so an SSL socket's TLS state is not touched from the watchdog thread) and falls back to `close()`. An aborted read no longer logs `upstream EOF`. The request takes the connection to cut under the same lock that records it, so a request made while data still flows cannot cut the fresh connection the fetcher opens in answer to it (found in review: the fetcher could flush and reconnect between the request and the abort). This covers a connection that is reading; one stuck while connecting or waiting for headers still waits out urlopen's 30s timeout, as before. The e2e stall test could not see this: it checks for the log line and for a reconnect, and the 30s timeout produced both.

- **The reservoir stays on the 188-byte grid across connection seams: a seam mid-packet used to put every later write to ffmpeg off it, for the rest of the tune.** `align_to_188()` (invariant #10, the 6.2.1 fix) aligns writes by counting bytes, which holds only while every byte before them is on the grid. A connection that ends mid-packet (an EOF lands wherever the edge stopped) or starts mid-packet (the code already allowed for it: "a TS may start mid-packet") left a remainder that shifted everything after it: with connection 1 ending 100 bytes into a packet, every packet written after the seam started off the grid, the same failure 6.2.1 fixed. Each connection now goes through a `PacketAligner`: it syncs on the first packet (three sync bytes 188 apart, as the parser does), drops the bytes before it, carries the unfinished last packet between reads and drops it when the connection ends. A connection that drops anything logs `upstream connection ended: dropped AB before its first TS packet and BB of an unfinished last packet` (file only), so the log now shows how often this edge actually cuts mid-packet. A connection with no TS sync in its first 94KB is not a TS at all and passes through as before (`upstream connection: no TS sync in its first NKB; passing it on unaligned`), so ffmpeg still fails on it loudly instead of the stream going quiet; a short answer without sync (an error text `non_ts_kind` does not know, a fragment under three packets) is passed on when its connection ends. Both are padded with `0xFF` to the next packet boundary when the connection ends, so whatever follows is back on the grid; ffmpeg skips the filler as it skips the junk before it. This also covers the stale-tail-after-flush case: everything a flush leaves behind is on the grid.

- **The stats line keeps coming while the reservoir is empty.** It was written after each released slice, and the pacer waited for data first, so an outage that drained the cushion to zero logged nothing: no `cushion=0s` line, exactly when it mattered. The pacer now wakes every 0.5s when there is nothing to release and writes the line on its 15s schedule regardless. A dry window counts as "no bytes flowing" for the #5 detector, so it ends a run of bad windows, as its rule always said.

- **ffmpeg dying on its own ends the stream.** Its death was noticed only by the next write to its stdin. With the reservoir empty there was none: an OOM-killed ffmpeg left the channel on dead air while the fetcher kept reconnecting, instead of the process ending so Dispatcharr retries or fails over. The stderr watcher now waits for ffmpeg when its stderr closes; if nothing asked it to stop, it logs `ffmpeg exited on its own (rc=N); shutting down` and ends the stream.

- **A full reservoir is not a stall.** When the reservoir reached `RESV_MAX_BYTES` the fetcher stopped reading on purpose, arrival froze, and the stall watchdog reconnected a healthy upstream every 90s. The watchdog's clock now restarts while the reservoir is full. (Reaching 256 MB takes a downstream that stops reading, so this was rare.)

- **The logged edge never carries `user:password@`.** `upstream connected edge=` took the network location straight from the URL. It is now `host[:port]` only.

- **Restructure, no behaviour change of its own.** About twenty module-level variables shared by four threads became objects: `Stream` owns one tune; `Upstream` is the fetcher thread with its reconnect requests, the stall watchdog and the corrupt-loop detector; `Reservoir` is the buffer and its lock; `ReplaySkip`, `Pacer`, `CorruptionDetector` and `PacketAligner` hold their own state. The parser counts the bytes fed to it instead of reading the fetcher's global. Settings stay module constants read at import. Log lines are unchanged except for the new ones above. Three test files used to check copies of inline logic (the release factor, the #5 window rule, the fetcher's flush block); they now call `release_factor()`, `CorruptionDetector` and `Upstream.request_reconnect()` / `take_request()`. `tools/parsecheck.py` no longer bumps a global to feed the parser.

- `docs/TELEMETRY.md` (the new lines, `upstream EOF`, the stall line, `in_total`), `docs/TUNABLES.md` (`RESV_STALL_S`), `docs/INVARIANTS.md` #10 and the README updated. Unit tests 191 → 227: `tests/unit/test_upstream_seams.py` (the aligner over odd read sizes, a seam mid-packet through the real fetcher and on through `align_to_188`, non-TS pass-through and its filler, a short error answer and a fragment mid-tune leaving the grid intact, a reconnect request never cutting the connection it causes, the edge without credentials, `abort_read()` and a forced reconnect against a real local edge that goes silent), `tests/unit/test_stream_lifecycle.py` (stats from a dry reservoir, everything in the reservoir reaching ffmpeg in order, a dead ffmpeg ending the stream and waking a waiting pacer, ffmpeg stopped by us not reported), plus a full reservoir not tripping the watchdog, the debounce open right after boot, the third identical corrupt dts, and the grace-floor edge.

## [6.3.12] — 2026-10-14

Telemetry ([@PilaScat](https://github.com/PilaScat)). **No change in behaviour** — the telemetry line gains two fields at the end, `vholes=N% aholes=N%`; pacing, the watchdogs and the ffmpeg command are untouched.

- **New `vholes=N%` and `aholes=N%` telemetry fields: the share of the window's content that the video, and the sound, is missing.** On 12 October 2026 (Dispatcharr 0.32.0, Jellyfin) a provider feed arrived at full rate (`in=2.07Mbps crate=2.05Mbps`) while dropping 4s out of every 8, video and sound: in 49s of the channel's output there were 732 audio packets and 584 video frames instead of ~1540 and ~1230. The viewer saw lag and the sound out of sync. Nothing flagged it: the PCR jumps with the content, so `in` matches `crate`; the jumps stay under ffmpeg's 10s `dts_delta_threshold`, so no `timestamp discontinuity` line; and ffmpeg's `speed=` stays at 1.0. The parser now reads the PAT and PMT to find the video and audio PIDs (audio: MPEG audio, AAC, AC-3, E-AC-3, or private data carrying an AC-3, E-AC-3 or AAC descriptor), takes the DTS (or the PTS) of every PES start, and for each track adds up the gaps longer than 0.5s against the span its timestamps cover; each telemetry line reports both shares for its window and starts the next one from zero. A track that sends nothing in a window while the other one covers at least 5s of content reads 100 (in production, before this floor, seven windows around reconnects read `aholes=100` on a sliver of video with no audio yet). PAT and PMT sections that span packets are put back together, sections marked not yet current are ignored, and a track that a complete PMT no longer lists is dropped, so a reconnect onto a video-only stream does not read `aholes=100`. A reconnect seam, a discontinuity flag and a jump of 10s or more are not counted; the flag usually sits on the PCR PID, often in a packet without payload, so a flag on the PCR PID or on either track excuses the next timestamp of both tracks. A PES header that a long adaptation field pushes into the next packet is read from both. The counters sit behind a small lock of their own, taken on each PES start and once per telemetry line, so a gap that lands while the window is read is not lost; both tracks share one window boundary. It is measured on the input, before ffmpeg, so a hole that `-af aresample=async=1` would pad with silence still shows. On the bench, 65s runs on `synth.ts` and on copies of it with packets removed: clean `0/0` in every window; 4s of video dropped out of every 8 `vholes` 50, 35, 51, 52 with `aholes=0`; 4s of sound dropped out of every 8 `aholes` 46, 51, 51, 51 with `vholes=0`; sound stopping 20s in `aholes=100` from the second window on. It is a measurement only: acting on it is left to whoever reads the log, as with `ccerr`.

- `docs/TELEMETRY.md` documents the fields. 30 new unit tests in `tests/unit/test_stream_holes.py` (PIDs from the PMT, private data with and without an audio descriptor, the 4-in-8 pattern on both tracks, audio holes told apart from video holes, a track that stops while the other runs, a source without audio, PTS without DTS, the window reset, seams, flagged discontinuities, far jumps, `synth.ts` whole and with video or sound removed, a PMT split over two packets, a cut-off PMT, a PMT that drops the audio, a discontinuity flag on the PCR PID, the lock, a PMT not yet current, a PES header split over two packets, a reconnect dropping a half-read header) and 5 e2e tests in `tests/e2e/test_stream_holes.py` (clean source, half the video missing, half the sound missing, sound stopping mid-stream, a forced EOF that must never read 100). `Run.stats_lines()` reads the new fields.

## [6.3.11] — 2026-10-14

Two new default-off tunables ([@PilaScat](https://github.com/PilaScat)). **No change for existing installs** — with `RESV_AUDIO_CODEC` and `RESV_AUDIO_FILL_GAPS` unset the ffmpeg command is byte for byte the 6.3.10 one.

- **New `RESV_AUDIO_CODEC=aac` tunable: the audio can be re-encoded to AAC instead of AC3.** Invariant #3 re-encodes to AC3 because Plex needs it (Dispatcharr #1122). Behind Jellyfin that choice costs twice, measured on a production install (Dispatcharr 0.32.0, Jellyfin, Plezy clients, 9–11 October 2026). 26 of the 40 ffmpeg processes Jellyfin started for live TV were `DirectStream` runs whose only work was converting the AC3 back to AAC for clients that do not take AC3. And two viewers with audio passthrough on, a Fire TV 4K Max with an eARC soundbar and an Android TV, re-tuned every 5–200s while the feed held its cushion and the reverse proxy delivered every tune at the content rate: one second after each tune their player logged `Invalid audio PTS: 4.96 -> 6.97`, then `AudioTrack underrun` and an A/V desync. In passthrough the AC3 goes to the soundbar undecoded and the player cannot absorb the 2s jump; with passthrough off it decodes and plays on. mpv never passes AAC through, so with `aac` every client decodes. `aac` is `-c:a aac -b:a 192k -ac 2 -ar 48000`. The re-encode stays, and with it the safety net of invariant #3 (broken source frames stop in ffmpeg's decoder, one codec and layout across failovers): `copy` is not accepted, and any value other than `ac3` or `aac` falls back to `ac3` with one line at startup (an empty value stays silent, as with `RESV_AUDIO_LANG`), `RESV_AUDIO_CODEC='copy' is not one of ac3, aac; encoding audio as ac3`.

- **New `RESV_AUDIO_FILL_GAPS=1` tunable: holes in the audio timeline are filled with silence**, with either codec, through `-af aresample=async=1` (a hole longer than 0.1s is padded, an overlap is trimmed). Measured on the bench with fixtures derived from `synth.ts`, AAC output, 60s runs: a source that drops 2s of audio passes a 2.02s hole without the tunable and none with it. Far jumps never reach the filter: the `mpegts` input treats jumps beyond `dts_delta_threshold` (10s) as discontinuities, and with the audio PTS moved 300s ahead in place (158 `timestamp discontinuity` lines, the shape a provider produces) the audio still ends with the video, at 67.29s both, with no hole. Audio that stops altogether at 41s does not hold the video, which runs on to 64s as without the tunable.

- `docs/TUNABLES.md`, `docs/INVARIANTS.md` #3 and the README diagram updated. 13 new unit tests in `tests/unit/test_audio_codec.py` (default command unchanged, `aac` arguments, fallback and its log line, an empty value kept silent, the filter with either codec, the `RESV_TS_CHANNEL` command). 6 new e2e tests in `tests/e2e/test_audio.py` on fixtures derived from `synth.ts`: AAC 48 kHz stereo output, a 2s hole kept without the tunable and closed with it in AC3 and AAC, video still flowing when the audio stops, no silence flood after a 300s in-place PTS jump. The tests pass both settings through `run_pipeline()`'s `extra_env`; `tests/e2e/harness.py` gains `ffprobe_audio_packets()`.

## [6.3.10] — 2026-10-12

New default-off tunable and a correction to where settings go. **No change for existing installs** — with `RESV_AUDIO_LANG` unset the ffmpeg command is byte-for-byte the one 6.3.9 ran.

- **New `RESV_AUDIO_LANG` tunable (issue #50, discussion #48).** A 3-letter ISO 639-2 code (`fra`) adds `-metadata:s:a:0 language=fra` to the output, so players list the audio track by language instead of "Unknown". The value is trimmed and lowercased; anything that is not three letters (`fr`, `francais`) is logged once and ignored, because ffmpeg's mpegts muxer writes no tag for it. It replaces a language the provider already sends. Deliberately **not** added: a general extra-ffmpeg-args setting (a second `-bsf:v` silently replaces the first, which would drop `dump_extra=freq=keyframe`, invariant #4), an audio-track selector (a missing index makes ffmpeg exit at startup, with a trailing `?` it silently produces no audio; no one has needed another track yet), and `title=` (MPEG-TS has no field for it, the muxer drops it). Embedded captions cannot be removed from a copied video stream without stripping all SEI, which also removes the recovery-point data mid-stream tune-in relies on, so that stays unsupported.

- **Docs correction: `RESV_*` variables set on the Dispatcharr container are not seen by the script.** The README, `docs/TUNABLES.md` and the plugin's tuning note said to set them on the container. Dispatcharr's `entrypoint.sh` starts its workers with `su - "$POSTGRES_USER" -c …`, which clears the environment, and re-exports only its own list of variables, so a Docker or Unraid variable never reaches reservoarr (discussion #46: the reporter was right). The documented way is a stream profile with command `/usr/bin/env` and parameters `RESV_X=value /data/reservoarr/reservoarr.py {streamUrl} {userAgent}`, as `RESV_TS_CHANNEL` already required. New "Where settings go" section in `docs/TUNABLES.md`; README, the plugin docstring and `tuning_note` point to it. It also says to make a separate profile, since **Generate Stream Profile** resets the command and parameters of a profile with the same name.

- New tests: `tests/unit/test_audio_lang.py` (default command unchanged; valid and invalid codes; invariant tokens present with and without the tunable; the `-output_ts_offset` splice still lands before `pipe:1`) and `tests/e2e/test_audio_lang.py` (the tag reaches the output TS). `run_pipeline` gained an `extra_env` argument.

## [6.3.9] — 2026-10-12

Three fixes. **No change for working installs** except the backoff reset below — a connection that carries a live TS is otherwise handled exactly as in 6.3.8.

- **An HLS playlist, HTML page or XML document as upstream is now an attempt without data, not a reconnect loop.** The fetcher took any `200` for a live TS. A stream URL that really is an `index.m3u8` (or an edge answering with an error page) delivered a few hundred bytes; those counted as data, reset the reconnect backoff to 1s and made `arrived_total` non-zero, so `RESV_GIVEUP_TRIES` could never fire. The edge was hit about once a second (a provider with a 2-connection cap feels that), prefill showed `0.0MB`, ffmpeg got junk, and the player never started (discussion #24: `reconnects=26`, `buf=0.0MB`). Now the first chunk of every connection is matched against `#EXTM3U`, `<!DOCTYPE`, `<html` and `<?xml` (leading whitespace ignored, case-insensitive). On a hit the bytes are dropped, the connection closed, and the fetcher logs `upstream sent an HLS playlist, not a live TS; counted as an attempt without data (reservoarr needs a raw MPEG-TS URL, see README)` (or `an HTML page` / `an XML document`); with `RESV_GIVEUP_TRIES` set the process then gives up as on a `403`. Matched on content, not `Content-Type` (CDNs label live TS `video/mp4`) and not on a missing `0x47` (a live TS may start mid-packet). Unlike `RESV_REJECT_MP4`, this check is always on: these answers can never be played. `RESV_REJECT_MP4` stays opt-in.

- **The reconnect backoff now resets only after a connection has carried real data** (one full 94 KB read chunk, 1 MB, or 10s connected), not on its first chunk. The old reset defeated its own comment ("an empty connect must keep backing off"): any short non-empty `200` reconnected every second. A healthy stream that reconnects after minutes of data is unaffected.

## [6.3.5] — 2026-09-14

New default-off tunable ([@PilaScat](https://github.com/PilaScat), [#40](https://github.com/brko7/reservoarr/issues/40) / [PR #42](https://github.com/brko7/reservoarr/pull/42)). **No change for existing installs** — `RESV_REPLAY_SKIP=0` is the default and leaves the output byte-for-byte as in 6.3.4.

- **New `RESV_REPLAY_SKIP=1` tunable that drops what an edge resends after a plain reconnect.** On some CDNs a connection ends with a clean EOF every few minutes and the next one starts 9–25s behind the point the old one reached. The fetcher appended that replay, so viewers saw the same seconds twice; and because `on_reconnect()` re-anchors the PCR chain, the replay was banked as new cushion on every reconnect instead of self-draining. With the tunable on, the fetcher remembers the last PCR taken before the seam and holds the packets of the new connection until the PCR passes it; only then is what came before dropped.   |---|---|---|
  | cushion | 20 → **42s** | 20 → 20 → 21s |
  | ffmpeg `timestamp discontinuity` | 1–3 per run | 0 |
  | log | — | `replay after reconnect: skipped 23.8s (7.1MB) already delivered` |

- **`RESV_GIVEUP_TRIES` also counts bytes arriving, not bytes ingested**, so an attempt that delivered only a replay is not an attempt without data.

- **The stall watchdog (#4) follows bytes arriving from upstream, not bytes ingested.** While the gate holds a replay, `in_total` does not move; an edge that delivered a replay at realtime for longer than `RESV_STALL_S` would have tripped a reconnect in the middle of it. The fake now hands `stderr_watcher` a real OS pipe opened the way `Popen(bufsize=0)` opens stderr (`io.FileIO` over the read fd) — without the fix, four of its tests fail with the production `AttributeError`.

## [6.3.1] — 2026-07-03

Hardening release from a full-repo review. Three runtime fixes in `reservoarr.py` plus one plugin install-gate fix. **No pacing/controller behaviour changes**; all invariants intact.

- **Monotonic clock for all interval math.** Pacing debt, the #4 stall watchdog, the per-class reconnect debounces, and the arrival-rate window previously measured intervals on `time.time()` (wall clock). A backward NTP step of T seconds became a single `time.sleep(T)` inside the pacing loop — if T exceeded the remaining cushion, the player starved and the tune died; a forward step could false-trip the stall watchdog into a spurious (buffer-kept) reconnect. All interval measurements now use `time.monotonic()`; the wall clock remains only in log-line timestamps. The debounce anchors initialize to `-inf` rather than `0.0` because monotonic time can be under 90s shortly after host boot, which would otherwise rate-limit the first forced reconnect on a freshly booted machine.

- **Clean shutdown when SIGTERM lands mid-pacing-loop.** `on_term` closes ffmpeg's stdin from the signal handler — deliberately, since that unblocks a write wedged on a full pipe. But when the signal lands between `next_slice()` and the write, the write executes against a *closed* file object and raises `ValueError`, which the `except (BrokenPipeError, OSError)` clause did not catch: every unlucky channel stop dumped a Python traceback into Dispatcharr's transcode log and exited nonzero. The window is a large fraction of loop wall-time (it includes the pacing sleep), so this was a routine occurrence, not a rarity. Now caught and logged as a normal shutdown; a `ValueError` raised while *not* shutting down still propagates — real bugs must stay loud.

- **Log rotation runs continuously, not only at spawn.** The 10 MB rotation check ran once at process start, so a single long-lived tune (a 24/7 channel) grew `delaybuf.log` unboundedly until the *next* channel start — contradicting what `docs/TELEMETRY.md` promised. The check now also runs every 512th log line (~2h at stats cadence, ~5min under an ffmpeg-stderr storm; worst-case overshoot past 10 MB is under 100 KB). The open-per-write append pattern keeps it safe across concurrent streams; a process losing the `os.replace` race just swallows the ENOENT. New regression tests in `tests/unit/test_log_rotation.py` (import-time rotation, mid-run rotation, below-threshold no-op).

- **Plugin upgrade gate: unknown installed version now reinstalls.** `plugin.py` treated a missing or unparseable `.installed_version` sentinel as "no upgrade needed". The sentinel write in `_install()` is `suppress(OSError)`-wrapped — if it ever failed once (transient read-only mount, disk full) while `reservoarr.py` existed, **no future version would ever auto-update the script, silently and permanently**: exactly the drift class invariant #11 exists to prevent. The gate now reinstalls whenever the local version is unknown; `_install()` is an idempotent copy, so the repair costs nothing and self-heals the sentinel on the next successful write.

- All 58 unit + 7 e2e tests pass. `docs/TELEMETRY.md` rotation note updated.

## [6.2.1] — 2026-06-21

Bug fix: TS-packet alignment on writes to ffmpeg's stdin.

- **`align_to_188(tail, chunk)`** module-level helper. The pacing loop now feeds ffmpeg only 188-byte multiples, carrying any unaligned tail across iterations. Previously, the loop wrote each `next_slice()` result `d` to `ff.stdin` directly. Because `r.read()` on the upstream HTTP socket returns arbitrary-length byte runs and the deque accumulates them whole, those writes did not reliably start at 188-byte TS-packet boundaries. ffmpeg's mpegts demuxer assumes they do, so partial-packet writes were detected as `timestamp discontinuity` events and (when the misalignment landed on AAC PES headers) as `channel element X.Y is not allocated` decoder errors that silently discarded audio frames. The viewer experienced the cumulative AAC frame discards as audio drifting out of sync with video over the course of a session.

  **Discovered 2026-06-16 evening** while debugging audio desync on channel 500157163 (kids' viewing). Diagnosis came from comparing live ffmpeg session telemetry to ffmpeg consuming the same captured upstream as a regular file: the offline run, which naturally reads in 188-aligned blocks, produced 0 disc lines / 30s; the live run, paced through the reservoir with arbitrary-length writes, produced 100+ disc lines / 30s on identical bytes. The provider's stream itself was clean.

  **Soak result:** deployed to tigar at 16:30 UTC on 2026-06-16; ran clean across multiple kid viewings (ccerr=0, pcrrej=0, no `would-fire` events, no audio desync) for 5 days before this release. The latent bug was present in every reservoir version since v5; it manifested as user-visible failures only when an unrelated provider-side stream variation (post-2026-06-16 source had AAC PES boundaries that landed on misaligned write boundaries more often) crossed the bug.

- **`tests/unit/test_ts_alignment.py`** — 17 regression tests covering: function existence (a revert that drops `align_to_188` fails loudly), every output is a 188-multiple, byte-perfect reconstruction across arbitrary chunk sequences, pathological one-byte chunks, empty inputs, parametrized realistic and adversarial chunk sizes, and a 2000-iteration randomized property check that the carried tail stays bounded below 188.

  No existing test would have caught this: the e2e suite uses synthetic fixtures whose AAC PES headers don't happen to land on the bug, and ground-truth comparisons key off ccerr/sync counters that are themselves fed misaligned bytes. The new unit tests target the alignment invariant directly.

**Implication for prior incident analysis:** earlier `iptv-incidents.md` entries that attributed AAC `channel element` errors and timestamp-discontinuity floods to provider-side corruption may have been measuring the bug's effects rather than (or as well as) real upstream damage. The 2026-06-16 #5-arming experiment in particular ran on bug-induced ccerr inflation; its "source-wide corruption, reconnect doesn't help" conclusion is therefore not reliable evidence against arming, and the open question remains open. Real provider corruption of course still exists — but it should be re-baselined against post-fix telemetry.

## [6.1.0] — 2026-06-16

Hardening release after the v6 wild corrupt-loop episode (2026-06-14, 08:03–08:06 UTC).

- **#1 lock-snapshot `released_total`** in `next_slice()`. `in_total` and `buf_bytes` both move under `cond` in the fetcher; reading them outside the lock was a torn-read source — telemetry-only impact today, but a sharp edge worth removing.
- **#2 sanitize `STREAM_ID`** — strip query strings and cap length, so a future provider URL shape can't leak an embedded API key into log filenames.
- **#3 nits** — argv guard with a clear usage message; `GRACE_S` made env-configurable as `RESV_GRACE_S`.
- **#4 silent-stall watchdog** (`RESV_STALL_S=25`, `0` to disable). No ingest progress for 25s while running → force a reconnect WITHOUT flushing the reservoir (buffer is good — grab a fresh front-load while the cushion keeps draining). Catches an open-but-silent socket that urlopen's 30s timeout would only surface after the cushion has drained.
- **#5 ingest-side TS-corruption detector** (`RESV_TS_RECONNECT`, default `0` = log-only). A sustained CC/sync error rate while bytes flow (`≥3 ccerr` per 15s for 2 consecutive windows) indicates a wedged/corrupt upstream; the rule fires ~30s before the ffmpeg-stderr corrupt-loop detector would. Calibrated on the 2026-06-14 incident: errors ramped from flat-zero to sustained `+5–10 ccerr/15s` 45s before the stderr detector. Critically, the rule catches *different-dts* corruption that the stderr same-dts-3× heuristic misses entirely (proven on 2026-06-16 when #5 fired 8× on a different-dts corruption episode the stderr detector would have ignored).
  - **Default is log-only** pending more arming evidence. Open question: does reconnecting to a fresh edge serve clean content (→ arming wins) or is the corruption source-wide (→ reconnect just adds blips)?
  - Arming test: set `RESV_TS_RECONNECT=1`, watch `ccerr` after a forced reconnect. Stops climbing → fresh edge was clean → keep armed. Continues → revert.
- **#6 clean forced-reconnect**. The previous `cur_response.close()` raised `AttributeError 'NoneType' has no 'read'` under TOCTOU → generic except → 1s backoff at the worst moment. Fixed: check `force_reconnect` inside the read loop, break clean.
- **F1 (pre-deploy review)** — per-class debounce (`last_forced_flush` / `last_forced_stall`) + sticky `flush_pending` under `cond`. A stall (no-flush) within the 90s debounce window must never suppress or downgrade a corrupt flush. 10/10 reconnect-precedence test.

Real-data validation: rule replay vs the real 2026-06-14 signature (fires 30s early, 0 false-positive on baseline/reconnect/gap); TsParser within 0.9% on a real TLC PCR capture; synthetic + real-capture cdn_sim e2e (no-regression cushion build, 12s stall absorbed, #4 fires at 25s no-flush, #6 clean reconnect, #5 no-FP, valid h264+ac3, controller self-drains EOF-overlap at 1.15×); 10/10 reconnect-precedence unit test.

**Deferred:**

- **#7 protect `crate` during corruption bursts.** During the 2026-06-14 burst the PCR-rate estimate degraded (2.16 → 1.28 Mbps), self-throttling recovery. The depression mechanism isn't reverse-engineerable from counters alone — needs a raw *corrupt* capture to build + validate. Grab raw bytes during the next corruption episode.

## [6.0.0] — 2026-06-13

**Headline rewrite.** v5's 30s cushion never actually built in production (logs showed 0–16s).

- **PCR content-rate pacing.** v5 paced against the measured *arrival* rate, which squandered the CDN's per-connection front-load burst (~30s of backlog served at line speed got released downstream at 5–7 Mbps chasing the inflated arrival rate) and could never rebuild the cushion at steady state (arrival averages exactly realtime; the rate window ended at the last arrival — excluding in-progress gaps, a ~4% overestimate that cancelled the 0.97 floor). v6 measures bytes-per-PCR-second on ingest with outlier rejection — banks the front-load surplus by construction on every connect, including reconnects after a corrupt-loop flush.
- **`TsParser`** on ingest: PCR clock (wrap-aware delta gate `0 < Δ < 10s`, re-anchor on discontinuity flags / reject runs / reconnects) and per-PID continuity counters (telemetry only in v6 — actions added in v6.1's #5).
- **Backoff-on-data fix.** v5 reset the upstream backoff on every `urlopen()` success — an empty-but-connectable upstream (dead channel) was hammered at 60 conn/min. v6 resets backoff only once bytes actually flow.
- **`cushion_s()` measured in PCR seconds**, not byte estimates.

Validated by 8-min live smoke (cushion 29s built in 15s, held 23–36s, `crate` pinned to true 5.37 Mbps), real-CDN capture parser within 0.2% of ffprobe truth, and the cdn_sim e2e absorbing 12s stalls.

## [5.0.0] — 2026-06-12

Initial delay buffer. The corrupt-loop detector was the highlight.

- **Eager fetcher + RAM reservoir + paced release** + **ffmpeg remux** (video copy + `dump_extra`, audio → AC3 per Dispatcharr #1122).
- **Auto-reconnect on upstream EOF.**
- **Corrupt-loop detector via ffmpeg-stderr watcher**: a wedged CDN connection can serve the same corrupt packet in a loop forever while a fresh connection is clean. Same `dts` reported 3× in 120s → force a reconnect + flush the poisoned reservoir (≤1/90s).
- **1.5 MB / 3s prefill sniff** (NOT a reservoir fill — Plex's tuner timeout is ~15s and isn't configurable).

Pacing was against the arrival rate — see v6.0.0 for why that didn't hold up.

## Pre-history

The script went through five offline prototypes before v5, killed by:

- ffmpeg `-re` sleeping on garbage DTS (25s output freeze with a full reservoir).
- Plex tuner bailing after ~15s initial silence (a 10s prefill caused "won't start, loads on retry").
- Pacing floor 0.85 starving the player to feed the reservoir.
- Buffering Timeout / Speed tuning being inert in Dispatcharr Proxy mode (stderr-parse-only detection).
